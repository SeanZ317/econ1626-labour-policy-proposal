# Supporting Clerical and Administrative Workers Through Australia's AI Transition

**Prepared for:** Australian Department of Employment and Workplace Relations (DEWR)  
**Policy focus:** Targeted support for clerical and administrative workers affected by generative AI

## Executive Summary

## Problem Definition and Policy Context

Prepared for the Australian Department of Employment and Workplace Relations (DEWR), this proposal addresses unequal adjustment costs from generative AI for clerical and administrative workers, particularly women and mature-aged employees. Their jobs contain routine information-processing tasks that generative AI can automate, but exposure is not equivalent to job loss: Jobs and Skills Australia finds that augmentation generally outweighs automation, while automation potential is concentrated in routine roles (Jobs and Skills Australia 2025). The early labour-market signal still warrants action. DEWR finds no broad AI-driven upheaval, yet occupations with greater automation exposure have experienced slower employment growth; it stresses that this association is suggestive, not causal (DEWR 2026). Acting early can reduce displacement and widening digital and gender gaps, while targeting is needed to avoid subsidising training unrelated to real job transitions. The proposal therefore operationalises Action 5 of the National AI Plan, which commits government to skills development, career support and workforce mobility, with particular attention to women and mature-aged workers (DISR 2025).
