# Reflection on the Use of AI

## Tools and purposes

I defined the policy question, selected DEWR and clerical and administrative workers as the audience and target group, and made the final decisions about scope, evidence and recommendations. I used OpenAI Codex as a supporting tool to locate official Australian and OECD sources, generate alternative structures, check calculations and improve Markdown and RMIT Harvard formatting. Its outputs were starting points that I reviewed rather than final answers.

## Example of accepting, modifying or rejecting AI output

My modification involved the implementation budget. A Codex draft changed the employer grant from $35,000 to $40,000 without justification. I compared it with the options table, rejected the change, restored $35,000 and recalculated 500 grants as $17.5 million. I assigned $12.5 million to administration and evaluation, preserving the $150 million total and ensuring it reflected my assumptions.

## Limitation or risk encountered

The main limitation was that fluent AI output could conceal unsupported claims, citation errors or weak causal reasoning. I checked statistics against ABS, Jobs and Skills Australia, DEWR, the Productivity Commission and OECD, matched in-text citations to references and labelled costs as illustrative. I also rewrote wording, selected the policy package and decided which evidence was persuasive. Git history records decisions, keeping authorship with me while AI supported research and editing.
